The number and the person.

BEYOND ETA uses an Indian auto-meter-inspired object and a customer phone to expose a translation. The phone converts events into arrival adjustments. The meter retains the time and conditions of the work. Press EXPOSE to see the inputs, decision and what this toy model leaves outside its number.

At the end, changing the reference estimate changes the delivery's verdict. Nothing about the journey changes. This evaluation rule is an artistic device, not a claim about how any particular platform judges workers.

What is real, and what is constructed?

Public engineering sources describe preparation, routing, traffic, waiting, rider availability and weather-related signals. These conditions are not universally absent from ETA systems. This artwork asks what is represented, how it is translated, and whose experience remains unexplained.

All times, adjustments and personal situations here are scripted. Exposure is not the same as added delay. No live sensors, stress measurement, pay calculation, ratings or penalties are connected. The work does not encourage rushing or treat a safety stop as a fault.

How to experience it

START begins the delivery. NEXT advances each chapter. EXPOSE opens and closes the model beneath the phone interface. At the rain chapter, expose the translation before continuing. On the final phone screen, compare judgement against the first and latest estimates. RESET returns to the beginning.
